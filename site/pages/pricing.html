{{icon:circle-help}} The boundary

Do I need a
commercial license?

The license text is the authority on where exactly the line falls. This is the plain-language version — if your situation is close to the line, read the license, or just ask.

{{icon:triangle-alert}} Yes, if Blacksite is used…

  • By or for a for-profit company
  • In paid employment or contracting work
  • To build, maintain, test, secure, or document a commercial product or service
  • For client work
  • In internal business operations
  • In CI, background automation, or unattended agent workflows for a business
  • By a commercial affiliate
  • For any activity with an anticipated commercial application

{{icon:circle-check-big}} No, generally, for…

  • Personal study and experimentation without anticipated commercial application
  • Hobby projects
  • Qualifying charitable organizations
  • Educational institutions
  • Public research organizations
  • Public safety or health organizations
  • Environmental protection organizations
  • Government institutions

The requirement applies even when Blacksite is not embedded in the resulting product, and even when the specific code you point it at does not generate revenue directly. Company size makes no difference.

{{icon:clock}} Evaluate before you buy

A company may evaluate Blacksite commercially for 30 consecutive calendar days under the Evaluation License, so you can find out whether it earns a place in your workflow before anyone raises a purchase order.

The period is defined at company level and starts when anyone first uses it on the company's behalf. Nonproduction internal testing only — no client work, no production use, no redistribution.

{{icon:globe}} Model costs are separate

Blacksite is bring-your-own-key. You connect your own Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, or AWS Bedrock account, and that provider bills you for tokens directly.

A Blacksite license covers the tool, not inference — and no request is proxied through a server of ours, because there isn't one.

{{icon:lock}} Source-available, not open source

The source is public so you can read exactly what the agent does with your code before you trust it with your repository.

A fork, clone, download, or contribution does not create a commercial license. Commercial rights exist only under a written license.
